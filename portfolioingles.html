Survival Guide

Amidst the problems presented, we have brought a survival guide/manual to help protect yourself.

Emergency Kit

• Water;
• Batteries;
• Flashlight;
• Non-perishable foods like canned goods and cereals;
• Radio;
• Blankets;
• Essential medications;
• Important documents.

Water Reserve

• Set aside 4L per person per day (increase the amount for elderly, pregnant women, or hot days).
• Use clean PET bottles or jugs without any residue of any product. Replace every 6 months.
• Boil for 3 to 5 minutes or use 2 drops of bleach per liter and let it sit for 30 minutes.
• Never drink water from floods or water with an unusual odor.

Security

• Keep a backpack ready with your documents, medications, flashlight, and some water.
• If you notice water rising quickly or hear cracks in the ground and walls, leave your house immediately.
• Never try to face the force of the water on the streets and stay away from windows and trees.
• If the situation worsens, call the Fire Department (193) or the Civil Defense (199).

First Aid

Airway Clearance


Ensure that the victim has an unobstructed airway. If necessary, tilt the head and lift the chin to open the airway. Carefully remove any visible object or secretion from the mouth that may be blocking the passage of air.

Breathing


Check if the victim is breathing by observing chest movement. If not, begin rescue breathing or, with the help of a healthcare professional, use an automated external defibrillator (AED) if available. Maintain a steady rhythm until the person starts breathing on their own or help arrives.

Circulation


Check for signs of circulation, such as a pulse in the neck or wrist. If none, begin chest compressions (30 compressions followed by 2 breaths). Position your hands in the center of the victim's chest and apply pressure using your own body weight.

Cuts and Lacerations


Clean the wound with running water and mild soap to prevent infections, apply firm pressure with a clean cloth to stop the bleeding, and cover with a sterile bandage. If blood soaks through the fabric, do not remove it; place another clean cloth on top and continue pressing.

Fractures


Immobilize the affected area with improvised splints, such as pieces of wood or folded magazines tied with cloth strips, and avoid moving the victim unnecessarily. Keep the joints above and below the injury completely fixed to prevent the bone from shifting further.

Burns


Cool the burned area with running cold water for at least 10 minutes, cover with a clean, damp cloth, and avoid applying creams, ointments, or home remedies. Never pop any blisters that form, as they serve as a natural barrier against severe infections.

Shock


Keep the victim warm with blankets, lying on their back with their legs elevated about 30 centimeters, and monitored until professional help arrives. Talk to the person to keep them calm and conscious, avoiding giving any type of food or drink.
