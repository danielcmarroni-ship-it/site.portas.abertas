Disasters

Are phenomena that negatively affect human society, bringing catastrophic consequences despite technological advancements aimed at predicting these events.

The unpredictability of many of these disasters is a defining characteristic, reflecting the complexity of natural processes.

Currently, the frequency and intensity of these phenomena have increased, which is directly related to global warming and environmental degradation.

These factors contribute to an imbalance in nature, resulting in a significant increase in natural disasters.

The impacts of natural disasters on human beings are varied and can cause substantial damage and loss.

These events not only affect people's lives but also have profound social and economic repercussions.

On the other hand, it is important to note that, for nature, these disasters play an essential role in the renewal and maintenance of ecosystems.

They aid in landform creation and the replenishment of natural water sources, showing that, despite the damage caused to human beings, natural disasters are also part of a natural cycle that sustains life on the planet.

Disaster Categorization:



Storms: Storms of rain, snow, hail, sand, and lightning, and they can be highly destructive depending on the amount of precipitation (torrential rains) and their intensity.
They can lead to catastrophic situations, such as landslides, avalanches, falling trees or power towers, among others.

Earthquakes and Tsunamis: also called seismic shocks, represent phenomena of sudden and temporary vibration of the Earth's surface that occur through the movement of tectonic plates, as well as volcanic activity and gas displacements inside the Earth.

Tsunamis are earthquakes that happen inside the seas, causing immense displacements of water.

Hurricanes, Cyclones, and Typhoons: phenomena intensified by air masses and, depending on the force they reach, they can devastate entire cities.

Drought: intensified by global warming, drought has become a problem faced by many groups around the world. Therefore, climate changes demonstrate that human actions are generating problems such as drought and consequently the expansion of the desertification process.

Volcanic eruptions: volcanic eruptions are dangerous insofar as the lava expelled by volcanoes is so hot that it can destroy communities, plants, and animals, depending on where they occur.

Floods: floods are natural phenomena, intensified by human action, which have been increasing significantly in recent decades.
An example is the excess of trash, which clogs storm drains, preventing the passage of water.

Floods, caused by increased rainfall and the prevention of drainage, cause landslides that can lead to the deaths of thousands of people, in addition to widespread destruction.
