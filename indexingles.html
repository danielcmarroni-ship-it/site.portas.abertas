How to protect yourself amidst global natural disruptions



Climate Changes

Warmer air can hold about 7% more moisture for every 1°C of global warming. This is why the world is witnessing such concentrated storms.

Tsunami

In the open ocean, tsunami waves travel at about 800 km/h, the same speed as a commercial jet airliner.

Earthquakes

Large earthquakes can help trigger volcanic eruptions, but only if the volcano is already full of magma under high pressure and on the verge of erupting.

El Niño

The name was coined by Peruvian fishermen in the 19th century because the warm water current used to appear off the coast of Peru near Christmas, in reference to the 'Christ Child.'

Climate Changes

With the extra vapor trapped in the atmosphere, modern storms not only produce more rain, but dump in a few hours the volume of water that should fall over entire weeks.

Tsunami

In the middle of the sea, a tsunami can be less than a meter high and pass unnoticed by ships; it only rises monstrously when it hits the coast and the water becomes shallow.

Earthquakes

Many people who have experienced major earthquakes report hearing a 'freight train' sound or a low rumble just before the main shaking begins.

El Niño

It has a direct opposite called La Niña, which causes an abnormal cooling of the waters in the equatorial Pacific Ocean, generating inverse climate impacts.

Climate Changes

The warming of the poles and glaciers alters global wind currents (such as jet streams), pushing cold fronts and rainbelts into regions where they did not typically hit with such force.

Tsunami

The word tsunami comes from Japanese (tsu = harbor, nami = wave), reflecting the fact that fishermen would return to the harbor to find everything destroyed, even though they had felt nothing in the open ocean.

Earthquakes

Extremely powerful earthquakes, like the one that struck Japan in 2011, release so much energy that they can shift the Earth's axis and shorten the length of a day by fractions of a microsecond.

El Niño

El Niño can intensify hurricanes and typhoons due to the changes in global climate conditions that it causes. During the occurrence of this phenomenon, surface waters in the Pacific Ocean warm significantly, affecting atmospheric patterns. This creates favorable conditions for the development and strengthening of tropical storms.
